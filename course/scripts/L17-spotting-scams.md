# L17 — Spotting scams and red flags

**Lesson ID:** L17
**Module:** 5 — Protect Yourself

**Learning objective:** Recognize the four classic scam red flags so you can spot and avoid bad actors targeting NIL athletes.

**Action step(s):** Adopt the rule: no unsolicited DM "investment opportunity" is ever real — delete and move on.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

The more visible you get, the more people come for your money. [pause] That's just the truth of having a name and a following. So this module is about keeping your money — and your name — safe. And it starts with learning to spot a scam from a mile away. Once you know the red flags, they're honestly kind of obvious. Let me give you the four big ones.

**Red flag number one: "guaranteed" returns.** [pause] Anybody who promises you guaranteed high returns is lying. Full stop. No legitimate investment can guarantee that — not one — because all real investing carries *some* risk. So the *instant* you hear "guaranteed returns," "can't lose," "risk-free profit"... that's not an opportunity, that's a flashing sign that says SCAM. Walk away.

**Red flag number two: pressure to act *right now.*** [pause] "This deal closes tonight." "Only a few spots left." "You have to send it in the next hour." That urgency? It's a manipulation tactic, on purpose. They rush you so you don't have time to think, or ask someone smart, or do any research. [pause] Here's your defense, and it always works: **a real opportunity can wait a day.** Always. If someone *won't* let you sleep on it, that *is* your answer. The pressure itself is the red flag.

**Red flag number three: you don't actually understand it.** [pause] Scammers *love* to make things sound complicated. Fancy words, confusing structures, "it's too advanced to explain simply." That confusion is a *tool* — if you don't understand it, you can't see the trap inside it. [pause] So flip it into a rule: if someone can't explain their thing in plain English that you fully get, the answer is no. Confusion is not sophistication. It's a warning.

**Red flag number four: they slid into your DMs.** [pause] This one's huge for athletes specifically. Be *extremely* skeptical of anyone reaching out *to you*, unsolicited, with an "opportunity." The crypto "coach." The "I help athletes grow their money" cold message. The stranger who's suddenly very interested in your finances. [pause] Real, legitimate help does not come hunting you in your DMs. It just doesn't. The unsolicited message is, by itself, a giant red flag.

Now stack them up. [pause] A DM, from a stranger, promising guaranteed returns, that you need to act on *right now* or you'll miss out? That's not three coincidences — that's a scam wearing every red flag at once. You'll see them coming now.

So here's your move, and make it a hard rule: **no unsolicited DM investment opportunity is ever real.** [pause] Ever. Don't reply, don't "just hear them out," don't get curious. Delete it and move on with your day. You lose *nothing* by ignoring it — because it was never real money to begin with.

Spotting strangers is the easy part, though. [pause] The *hard* part is when the person asking for money is someone you actually love. That's the next lesson, and it's a big one.
