# L08 — Hiring a CPA (and exactly what to ask)

**Lesson ID:** L08
**Module:** 2 — Taxes First

**Learning objective:** Understand why a CPA is the one pro worth paying early, what it costs, and exactly what to ask when hiring one.

**Action step(s):** Before your first real tax season with NIL income, budget ~$200–400 for one CPA session and save the list of questions to ask.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

Here's a line I want you to remember from this whole course: **a CPA is the one professional worth paying for early.** [pause] Not a money manager. Not an advisor taking a cut of your investments. A CPA — a tax pro — when you've got real NIL income. Let me tell you why, and exactly how to hire one without overthinking it.

First, what *is* a CPA? [pause] CPA stands for Certified Public Accountant. In plain English: a licensed tax expert. Their whole job is knowing the rules you don't have time to learn — making sure you pay what you owe, but *not a dollar more*, and that you don't accidentally mess something up that costs you later.

Now, why pay for one when there's free tax software? [pause] Because self-employment taxes — the 1099s, the quarterly payments, the deductions we just covered — get complicated fast, and the *mistakes* are expensive. A good CPA will usually **find you more in savings and dodged penalties than they charge.** [pause] Picture it: you pay them three hundred bucks, and they find you eight hundred in deductions you'd have missed and keep you from a penalty. You didn't spend three hundred. You *made* five. That's the math on a good CPA.

What's it cost? [pause] For a young athlete with a fairly simple situation, figure roughly **two hundred to four hundred dollars** for a session to get you set up and your taxes handled right. Prices vary, but that's a realistic ballpark. Compared to what they save you — and the stress they delete — it's one of the best deals in this entire course.

So when do you get one? [pause] Simple: **before your first tax season with real NIL income.** Don't wait until April when you're panicking. Reach out earlier — even now — so they can help you set things up *right* from the start.

Now, the most important part — **what to actually ask.** Walking into a pro's office can feel intimidating, so here's your cheat sheet. [pause]

Ask: **"Have you worked with self-employed people or 1099 income before?"** You want someone who knows this world, not just W-2 employees.

Ask: **"What's your flat fee for my situation?"** Get a clear number up front. No surprises.

Ask: **"How much should I be setting aside for taxes, given what I earn?"** Let them fine-tune your thirty-percent rule to *your* exact numbers.

Ask: **"What expenses can I deduct, and how should I track them?"** Free, personalized money-saving advice.

And ask: **"Do I need to make quarterly payments, and can you tell me how much?"** That's the timing piece, solved.

Bonus question if your income's growing: **"Is it worth setting up an LLC yet?"** We'll touch LLCs later, but your CPA is exactly the right person to answer it. [pause]

Write those down or screenshot this. That short list turns a scary meeting into a productive one.

So — that's the whole tax module. [pause] You know the money's untaxed, you set aside thirty percent the day it lands, you keep your 1099s and receipts, you know the quarterly dates, and you bring in a CPA to make it all easy. Taxes? Handled. Now let's go build your foundation.
