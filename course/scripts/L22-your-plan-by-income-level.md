# L22 — Your plan by income level

**Lesson ID:** L22
**Module:** 6 — Your Year-One Plan

**Learning objective:** Tailor the order of operations to your specific income tier (~$1k–$10k, ~$10k–$50k, $50k+).

**Action step(s):** Find your income tier and follow the specific tailored checklist for it.

**Runtime estimate:** ~6 min

---

## Voiceover script

The order of operations is the same for everyone. [pause] But *how much* you focus on each piece — and which pros you bring in — depends on how much you're earning. So let's get specific. I'm going to walk through three income levels. Find yours, and follow that plan. The steps are the same; the *emphasis* changes.

Let's start with **earning roughly one thousand to ten thousand dollars a year.** [pause] This is a huge chunk of NIL athletes, and honestly, this is where good habits get *built.* Here's your plan. [pause] Taxes set-aside is *still* number one — yes, even on small amounts. Build that thousand-dollar emergency fund. Open the Roth IRA and invest even *twenty-five to fifty dollars a month.* [pause] And listen — at this level, the *habit* matters way more than the amount. You're not getting rich off fifty bucks a month right now; you're building the *muscle* that makes you rich later. And one more thing: at this level, do *not* pay anyone a percentage to manage this for you. Your situation is too simple to need it — paying a manager here would be all cost, no benefit. You've got this yourself.

Now — **earning roughly ten thousand to fifty thousand a year.** [pause] This is the core of who this course is built for. Here's where it steps up. [pause] Everything from the first level still applies, but *bigger* — bigger tax set-asides, and build that emergency fund all the way up to a full three-to-six months. And here's the new non-negotiable: **see a CPA before your first tax season.** At this income, that's not optional anymore — the taxes are real enough that a pro will genuinely save you money and stress. Max out your Roth IRA contributions if you can. Start a real receipts-and-expenses system for those deductions. And ask your CPA the question: *"Is it worth setting up an LLC yet?"* Let the pro tell you.

And finally — **earning fifty thousand dollars a year or more.** [pause] First of all — congratulations, that's serious money. And serious money means serious discipline. Here's your plan. [pause] Everything above, *plus*: a **CPA is now mandatory**, full stop, and those quarterly estimated payments *really* matter at this level. Consider a **one-time, flat-fee financial planner** — a fee-only fiduciary, remember — for a personalized plan. Pay them by the hour or a flat fee, *never* a percentage of your money. Get *every* significant contract reviewed by a lawyer before you sign. [pause] And here's the big mindset piece: the bigger the money gets, the *louder* the temptation to blow it, and the *more* people show up wanting a piece. At this level, your boring, disciplined plan isn't just smart — it's your actual *edge* over every athlete who fumbled theirs.

So notice what *didn't* change across all three. [pause] Taxes first, every time. Emergency fund, every time. Boring index investing in a Roth, every time. Protect yourself, every time. The *foundation* is identical at every income level — that's the whole point. What scales up is the size of the set-asides and how many pros you loop in. [pause] The fundamentals never change. That's what makes this so doable.

So your move: **find your tier** — one-to-ten-K, ten-to-fifty-K, or fifty-K-plus — **and follow that specific plan.** [pause] Be honest about where you actually are right now, and do the version that fits. As you earn more, you just graduate up to the next tier. Same staircase, bigger steps.

One lesson left — and it's the one that determines whether *any* of this actually works: staying consistent. Let's finish.
