# L06 — Self-employment tax and the 1099 form

**Lesson ID:** L06
**Module:** 2 — Taxes First

**Learning objective:** Understand the three taxes you may owe (federal, state, self-employment) and what a 1099 form is and why you must report it.

**Action step(s):** Know to expect 1099 forms from anyone who paid you $600+, and keep them. Understand why the ~15.3% self-employment tax makes the 30% set-aside necessary.

**Runtime estimate:** ~5 min

---

## Voiceover script

Okay, last lesson I told you to set aside thirty percent. Now let me show you *why* it's that high — because once you see where the money goes, that number makes total sense. [pause]

When you earn NIL money, there are up to **three** taxes in play. Let's meet all three.

**Number one: federal income tax.** [pause] This is the big national one. The rate depends on your total income for the year — more income, higher rate. Standard stuff, applies to basically everyone.

**Number two: state income tax.** This one depends on where you live. Some states take a chunk. And a handful of states — like Florida and Texas — have *no* state income tax at all. So depending on your state, this one ranges from "a real bite" to "zero."

**Number three — and this is the surprise nobody sees coming. Self-employment tax. Around fifteen-point-three percent.** [pause] Let me explain this one, because it's the reason the set-aside is so high.

Remember how at a normal job, your employer quietly handles your taxes? [pause] Well, there's a specific tax — it funds Social Security and Medicare — and at a normal job, your employer pays *half* of it for you. You cover one half, they cover the other. You never even notice.

But you're self-employed now. There's no employer to split it with. [pause] So *you* cover the *whole* thing. Both halves. That's the fifteen-point-three percent. It's not a punishment — it's just that the "other half" your boss used to pay is now on you too. [pause] *That's* the surprise expense, and that's why thirty percent off the top is the smart play. Federal, plus maybe state, plus that fifteen-point-three — it adds up fast.

Now — the form. Meet the **1099.** [pause]

A 1099 is just a tax form a company sends you at the end of the year reporting how much they paid you. The rule of thumb: any company that paid you **six hundred dollars or more** will usually send one. You might get several — one from each brand or deal.

But here's the part you *cannot* miss. [pause] When a company sends *you* a 1099... they send a **copy to the IRS at the same time.** So the government already knows about that income. It's on record whether you report it or not. [pause] Which means there's only one move: **always report all of it.** Every dollar. Even cash deals, even small ones, even the one you think slipped through. Trying to hide income the IRS already has a copy of is how a small tax bill turns into a big, scary problem. Not worth it. Ever.

So — keep every 1099 that shows up. Toss them in a folder, snap a photo, whatever works. They're the receipts you hand your tax person — which, spoiler, is the next lesson.

Quick recap. [pause] Three taxes: federal, maybe state, and the fifteen-point-three percent self-employment one that catches everybody. The 1099 reports what you were paid, the IRS gets a copy, so you always report everything. Thirty percent set aside covers it. You've got this.
