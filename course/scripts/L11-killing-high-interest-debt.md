# L11 — Killing high-interest (credit card) debt

**Lesson ID:** L11
**Module:** 3 — The Simple Foundation

**Learning objective:** Understand why paying off high-interest debt beats investing, and how to prioritize debts by interest rate.

**Action step(s):** List every debt and its interest rate. Attack anything above ~8–10% (especially credit cards at 20%+) before investing.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

Last foundation step, and it might be the most powerful one. [pause] If you've got high-interest debt — and for most people that means **credit card debt** — paying it off is literally the best "investment" you can make. Let me prove that to you, because once you see it, you'll want to attack it today.

Here's the key idea most people never get explained. [pause] When you carry a balance on a credit card, you're paying *interest* — and credit card interest is brutal. Often **twenty percent a year or more.** That means every year you carry that debt, the balance grows by twenty-plus percent if you don't knock it down. It's like a hole in your pocket that gets *bigger.* [pause]

Now connect that to investing. [pause] In the next module, we'll talk about growing your money in the market. A good, realistic long-term *return* on investments is, say, somewhere around seven percent a year on average. Solid. That's how wealth gets built.

So look at the two numbers side by side. [pause] Your credit card is *costing* you twenty percent. Your investments might *earn* you seven. [pause] If you invest while carrying that card balance, you're earning seven on one hand and *losing* twenty on the other. You're going backwards. That's why paying off the card comes *first.*

Here's the reframe that makes it click: **paying off a twenty-percent debt is a guaranteed twenty-percent return.** [pause] Guaranteed. No risk. No market dip can take it away. There is *no investment on earth* that reliably, safely hands you twenty percent. But paying off that card does *exactly* that. Every dollar you throw at a twenty-percent debt is a dollar earning you a guaranteed twenty percent. That's unbeatable.

Picture it. [pause] You've got two thousand on a card at twenty percent. Left alone, that's costing you around four hundred dollars a *year*, just in interest, for nothing. Pay it off, and you've essentially "earned" that four hundred back, guaranteed, forever. *That's* why it beats investing the same two thousand.

Now — not all debt is the emergency. [pause] The villain here is *high-interest* debt: credit cards, payday loans, that kind of thing. **Low-interest** debt — like some student loans at a low rate — is way less urgent. You don't have to panic over a four-percent student loan; that's a more relaxed, later conversation. The line in the sand is roughly **eight to ten percent.** Anything above that, treat it as a fire to put out *now.* Below that, less urgent.

So here's your move, and it's two parts. [pause] **One: list every debt you have and the interest rate next to each one.** Seeing them all in one place is half the battle. **Two: anything above eight to ten percent — especially those twenty-percent cards — attack it before you put a dollar in the market.** Pay it down aggressively. Kill it.

Do that, and your foundation is *complete.* Taxes handled, emergency fund growing, savings earning interest, high-interest debt dead. [pause] You are now genuinely ready for the fun part — making your money grow. That's the next module. Let's go.
