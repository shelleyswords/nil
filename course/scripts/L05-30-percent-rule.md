# L05 — The 30% rule: set money aside the day it lands

**Lesson ID:** L05
**Module:** 2 — Taxes First

**Learning objective:** Learn the 25–35% set-aside rule of thumb (use 30%) and the system of moving it to a separate account the moment money arrives.

**Action step(s):** Open a separate "Taxes" savings account, and commit to moving 30% of every NIL dollar into it the day it lands.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

Last lesson, the big idea: the money arrives whole, but it's not whole yours. [pause] This lesson, we make that practical with one number and one system. Both are simple. Both are kind of magic.

The number first. **Set aside between twenty-five and thirty-five percent of every NIL dollar for taxes.** When in doubt? Use **thirty percent.** [pause] That's your rule of thumb. Thirty percent off the top, every single time, no exceptions.

Why a *range*? Because your real tax rate depends on stuff — how much total you earn, what state you live in, your other income. Some of you will owe a little less, some a little more. But thirty percent is the safe, simple middle. [pause] And here's the beautiful part: if you set aside thirty and it turns out you only owed twenty-five — congrats, you've got a little bonus left over. That's a way better surprise than owing money you don't have.

Let me make it real. [pause] A ten-thousand-dollar deal lands. Thirty percent is three thousand. So the *second* that money hits, three thousand is not yours — it's the government's, you're just holding it. Seven thousand is what you actually get to work with. A five-hundred-dollar deal? A hundred and fifty goes to taxes, three-fifty is yours. Same move, every time, no matter the size.

Now the system — because a rule you don't act on is worthless. [pause] Here it is: **open a totally separate savings account, just for taxes.** Nickname it "Taxes" or "Not Mine" or "Do Not Touch" — whatever keeps your hands off it. And the *day* any NIL money lands, you move your thirty percent straight into that account. [pause] Then you forget it exists. Seriously. Pretend that account is invisible. When tax time comes, the money's already sitting there waiting. You don't scramble, you don't panic, you don't owe a cent you didn't plan for.

The reason this works is psychological. [pause] If the tax money is sitting in your *main* account, mixed in with everything else — you *will* spend it. Not on purpose. It just blends in and disappears. But money you physically moved to a separate, named account? That money's out of sight, out of mind, and safe. The separation is the whole trick.

So two action steps. One: open that separate "Taxes" account — many high-yield savings accounts work great for this, and we'll cover those soon. Two: make the unbreakable rule — thirty percent moves over the *day* the deal pays. [pause]

Do this one thing and you have basically defeated tax-season stress forever. Next, let's talk about *why* that number's so high — and meet the form that shows up in your mailbox.
