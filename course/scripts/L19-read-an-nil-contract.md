# L19 — How to read an NIL contract

**Lesson ID:** L19
**Module:** 5 — Protect Yourself

**Learning objective:** Know the four key things to check in any NIL contract (exclusivity, length, usage rights, deliverables) and when to get a paid review.

**Action step(s):** For any meaningful contract, check the four red-flag areas and get a paid review before signing.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

Every NIL deal is a *contract.* [pause] And a contract is just a set of promises you're legally agreeing to. The problem is, they're written in dense legal language that's *designed* to make your eyes glaze over so you just sign. We're not doing that. I'm going to give you four things to look for in *any* NIL contract — plain English — so you know what you're actually agreeing to.

**Thing one: exclusivity.** [pause] This is the big one people miss. Exclusivity means — does signing *this* deal stop you from working with *other* brands? [pause] Say you sign with one energy drink. Buried in the contract, it might say you can't promote *any* other drink — sometimes any competitor at all — for the length of the deal. So a small deal now could block a *much bigger* deal later. Always ask: "Does this lock me out of other deals? Which ones? For how long?"

**Thing two: length.** [pause] How long are you actually locked in? A month? A year? Three years? [pause] A great rate can turn into a *bad* deal if it ties you up for years while your value is climbing. The longer the commitment, the more carefully you read. Know your exit, know your end date.

**Thing three: what they can actually use.** [pause] Remember, NIL stands for *name, image, and likeness* — that's literally *you.* So the contract spells out how *they* get to use you. Your name, your face, your photos — for what, where, and for how long? [pause] Watch for deals that grab the right to use your image *forever*, or in ways you never pictured. You want to know *exactly* what you're handing over.

**Thing four: what *you* have to deliver.** [pause] What are you actually on the hook to do? How many posts? How many stories? Any appearances, any events? [pause] You want this crystal clear *before* you sign, so you're not suddenly drowning in obligations you didn't realize you agreed to — or accidentally breaking the contract by under-delivering.

So that's your four-point scan: **exclusivity, length, usage rights, and deliverables.** [pause] Run any contract through those four and you already understand it better than most athletes do.

But here's the most important move of all. [pause] If a deal is more than just pocket change — if there's real money or a real time commitment on the line — **get someone who knows contracts to look at it before you sign.** A short, paid contract review — a few hundred dollars, maybe — is *nothing* compared to getting trapped in a bad multi-year deal. [pause] Think about it: would you rather spend a couple hundred bucks now, or be stuck for three years in a contract that blocks every better opportunity? A paid review *every* time on meaningful deals. It's cheap insurance.

So your move: for any contract that matters, **scan the four things — exclusivity, length, usage, deliverables — and get a paid review before you sign.** [pause] Never sign in a rush, never sign because they're pressuring you — remember that red flag from two lessons ago. Real deals wait for your lawyer.

Last protection lesson is next: when you *actually* need to pay a professional, and exactly what that should cost. Let's wrap this module.
