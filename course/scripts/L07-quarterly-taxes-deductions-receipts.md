# L07 — Quarterly taxes, deductions & keeping receipts

**Lesson ID:** L07
**Module:** 2 — Taxes First

**Learning objective:** Understand quarterly estimated taxes (and the four dates), how legitimate business deductions lower your bill, and why keeping receipts matters.

**Action step(s):** Put the four quarterly tax dates in your phone calendar, and start a simple receipts folder (physical or app) for business expenses.

**Runtime estimate:** ~5 min

---

## Voiceover script

Two more tax things and then we bring in a pro to make it all easy. First: *when* you pay. Second: how to *lower* what you owe. Both matter. [pause]

Let's start with timing — and this trips people up. [pause] Because nobody's withholding taxes from your NIL money throughout the year, the IRS doesn't want to wait until April to get paid. They want their money *as you earn it.* So they ask you to pay **estimated taxes four times a year.** These are called **quarterly taxes.**

Roughly, the deadlines land in **April, June, September, and January.** [pause] Four check-ins a year instead of one big April reckoning. And here's the why-you-care: if you're earning real NIL income and you skip these, you can get hit with **penalties** — extra money owed on *top* of the tax, just for paying late. Annoying, and totally avoidable.

Now, real talk: if your NIL income is small, this might not bite you much. But the second you're earning real money, quarterly payments matter — and figuring out exactly *how much* to send each quarter is precisely the kind of thing your tax pro handles. [pause] For right now, your only job is: **put those four dates in your phone calendar so they never sneak up on you.** April, June, September, January. Done.

Okay — now the *fun* tax topic. Yes, there's a fun one. **Deductions.** This is how you legally pay *less.* [pause]

Because you're self-employed, **legitimate business expenses can lower the income you get taxed on.** Think about what you actually spend to *make* your NIL money. [pause] Equipment for content. Travel to an appearance or a shoot. A portion of your phone bill, since you're running your brand off it. Agent or marketing costs. Editing software. Those can count as business expenses — and business expenses shrink your taxable income, which shrinks your tax bill.

Quick example. [pause] Say you earned ten thousand, but you spent a thousand on legit business stuff to earn it — a ring light, some travel, software. You might only get taxed on *nine* thousand, not ten. That thousand in expenses just saved you real money. That's the game.

But — and this is the catch — **you have to be able to prove it.** [pause] Which means: **keep every receipt.** This is the boring habit that pays you back. You do *not* need fancy software. A shoebox works. A folder on your phone works. A free expense app works. The system doesn't matter; *doing it* matters. Snap a photo of the receipt the moment you get it, drop it in one place, move on.

So here's your move, two parts. [pause] One: those four quarterly dates — April, June, September, January — in your calendar right now. Two: start a receipts folder today, physical or digital, and feed it every business expense going forward. [pause]

That's almost everything on taxes. There's just one more piece, and honestly it's the smartest money you'll spend all year: hiring a tax pro. Next lesson.
