# L16 — What to avoid: meme stocks, crypto gambling, hot tips

**Lesson ID:** L16
**Module:** 4 — Investing Without Getting Ripped Off

**Learning objective:** Recognize the difference between investing and gambling, and avoid meme stocks, crypto gambling, day-trading, hot tips, guaranteed-return promises, and products you don't understand.

**Action step(s):** Commit to never risking your foundation on speculation; if you ever gamble, use only money you can afford to lose.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

You just built the *right* way to invest. [pause] This lesson is about the things that can quietly *undo* all that good work — the traps that look like investing but are really just *gambling* with extra steps. Let me show you what to dodge, so you don't blow up the foundation you just built.

Here's the big distinction, and it's everything: there's a difference between **investing** and **gambling.** [pause] Investing is the boring stuff we just covered — own a little of everything, cheaply, for a long time. Gambling is trying to get rich quick on one risky bet. They *feel* similar because both involve money and a screen. They are *not* the same. One builds wealth slowly and reliably. The other is entertainment that occasionally pays out — and usually doesn't.

So let's name the traps. [pause]

**Meme stocks.** [pause] Those stocks that blow up on social media, everybody's hyping, "it's going to the moon." By the time *you* hear about it, the people who made money are already selling — to you. It's musical chairs, and you do *not* want to be standing when the music stops.

**Crypto gambling.** [pause] Now — I'm not here to tell you crypto is evil. But there's a huge difference between a tiny, thoughtful position and *gambling* — throwing real money at random coins because someone in your DMs swears it's the next big thing. The second one is just a casino on your phone.

**Day-trading.** [pause] Buying and selling fast, all day, trying to outsmart the market minute by minute. It looks exciting and active and smart. The reality? The overwhelming majority of people who try it *lose money.* Remember — boring and slow beats exciting and fast.

**Hot tips.** [pause] "My guy says this one's about to pop." A tip from a friend, a cousin, some influencer. If it sounds like a secret shortcut to fast money... it's not. Real investing has no secret shortcuts.

Now here are two flashing-red warning signs to *run* from. [pause] **One: anything promising guaranteed high returns.** That is the number-one sign of a scam — and we've got a whole module on scams coming up. No legitimate investment guarantees you big returns. None. **Two: anything you can't explain simply.** If you can't describe how it works in one plain sentence, you don't understand it — and you should *never* put money in something you don't understand. Confusion is how people get separated from their money.

So what's the rule? [pause] It's simple. **Never, ever risk your foundation** — your tax money, your emergency fund, your retirement — on any of this stuff. That foundation is sacred. [pause] *If* you ever want to mess around with a meme stock or a little crypto, here's the only safe way: use **only money you could light on fire and be totally fine.** Money you can completely afford to lose. Treat it like the cost of a fun night out, not like investing. And keep it tiny.

That's it for the investing module. [pause] You know how to do it right — boring index funds, in a Roth, on autopilot — and now you know exactly what to avoid so you don't undo it. Next module, we protect everything you're building: scams, "friends" with deals, contracts, and shady people. Let's keep your money *yours.*
