# L02 — The 30-day rule (beating lifestyle creep)

**Lesson ID:** L02
**Module:** 1 — Mindset: Don't Fumble the First Check

**Learning objective:** Understand lifestyle creep and use the 30-day rule to stop the excitement of new money from turning into permanent expenses.

**Action step(s):** Adopt a personal rule: no big purchases for 30 days after any new deal. Note it in your phone.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

Let's talk about the sneakiest way money disappears. It's not one big dumb purchase. It's a hundred small "upgrades." [pause] It's called **lifestyle creep**, and it gets almost everybody.

Here's how it works. You get money, so you "level up." Nicer apartment. Eating out more. The newer phone. A subscription here, a subscription there. None of it feels reckless. Each one feels *earned*. [pause] But every single one of those upgrades quietly becomes a new bill that shows up *every month, forever* — long after the excitement wore off.

That's the trap. The car loses value the second you drive it. The index fund — which we'll get to — *grows*. Every dollar you pour into looking richer is a dollar that can't grow into actually *being* richer.

So how do you beat it? [pause] One stupidly simple rule. The **30-day rule.**

Here it is: **after any new deal, you make zero big purchases for 30 days.** That's it. The money can land. You can look at it. You just don't *spend* it on anything major for a month.

Why thirty days? Because the enemy isn't *you* — it's the *excitement*. [pause] New money creates this little high, this urge to celebrate, to treat yourself, to show people. And that high makes terrible decisions look brilliant. Thirty days lets the hype cool all the way down. After a month, if you still want the thing — and you can actually afford it *after* taxes and savings — fine, maybe. But nine times out of ten? The urge is just... gone. You dodged it for free.

Picture this. [pause] A five-thousand-dollar deal hits. Day one, you're *this* close to dropping eighteen hundred on a designer everything because you feel amazing. The 30-day rule says: not today. Just wait. By week three, that fire's basically out, and that eighteen hundred is still sitting there — ready to go toward your taxes, your emergency fund, your future. You didn't deprive yourself. You just let the excitement expire before it cost you.

And let me be clear — this isn't about never having nice things. [pause] It's about making sure *you* decide, with a clear head, instead of the dopamine deciding for you. Big difference.

One rule. Thirty days. Put it in your phone right now: "No big buys for 30 days after a deal." Future you is gonna be so glad you did.
