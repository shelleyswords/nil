# L20 — When you actually need a pro (and what to pay)

**Lesson ID:** L20
**Module:** 5 — Protect Yourself

**Learning objective:** Know which three pros you might actually need (CPA, fee-only planner, lawyer), how to hire without overpaying, and the magic question.

**Action step(s):** Save the magic question — "Are you a fee-only fiduciary, and how exactly do you get paid?" — and use it before hiring anyone.

**Runtime estimate:** ~5 min

---

## Voiceover script

Let's clear up a big confusion. [pause] You do *not* need a full-time money manager taking a cut of everything you own. For a young athlete with a simple setup, that's overkill — and remember the fee trap, it's expensive overkill. But there *are* a few specific pros worth paying, at the right moments, the right way. Let me show you exactly who, when, and what it should cost.

There are basically **three** pros you might want. [pause]

**One: a CPA — a tax pro.** [pause] We covered this in the taxes module, but it bears repeating: this is the one worth paying *early.* As soon as you've got real NIL income, a CPA saves you more than they cost. That's your first hire.

**Two: a fee-only, flat-rate financial planner.** [pause] Notice what I did *not* say — I did not say a money manager who takes a percentage of your money every year. I said **fee-only, flat-rate.** This is someone you pay a *set* fee — by the hour or a flat amount — for a *one-time* plan, when your situation actually gets complex. They give you a roadmap and walk away. They do *not* take an ongoing cut of everything you own. Big, big difference. You'd want this maybe when your income gets large or complicated — not for a simple Roth-and-index-fund setup you can run yourself.

**Three: a lawyer.** [pause] Specifically, to review significant contracts — like we just talked about. For any deal with real money or a long commitment, a lawyer's review is cheap insurance against an expensive mistake.

So that's the *who.* Now — the *how*, so you never get fleeced. [pause]

Here's the single most valuable thing in this entire module. **The magic question.** Memorize it, save it, tattoo it wherever. Before you hire *anyone* who touches your money, you ask: [pause] **"Are you a fee-only fiduciary, and how exactly do you get paid?"**

Let me break down why that one sentence is so powerful. [pause] **"Fiduciary"** means they're *legally required* to act in *your* best interest — not theirs. **"Fee-only"** means they're paid *only by you* — not by commissions from selling you stuff. So you're asking, in one shot: are you legally on my side, and are you free of the temptation to sell me junk for a commission? [pause] And then — "how *exactly* do you get paid?" — you make them say an actual number, out loud, in dollars.

Here's the magic part. [pause] If they give you a clear, confident, straight answer — "Yes, I'm a fee-only fiduciary, my flat fee is X dollars" — great, that's an honest pro. But if they get *vague*, if they dodge, if they start talking around it... [pause] that fuzziness *is* your answer. **Walk away.** Honest pros are happy to tell you how they're paid. Only the ones with something to hide get squirmy.

A couple more quick rules. [pause] Always **prefer flat fees or hourly** over "a percentage of my money." And **get it in writing** — the cost, the service, all of it. If anyone's vague about how they're paid, you already know what to do.

So your move: **save the magic question in your notes right now** — "Are you a fee-only fiduciary, and how exactly do you get paid?" — and *use* it before you ever hire anyone. [pause]

That's the whole Protect Yourself module. You can spot scams, you can say no to loved ones, you can read a contract, and you can hire a pro without getting ripped off. Your money — and your name — are safe. [pause] Now let's bring *everything* together into your actual year-one plan. Final module. Let's finish this.
