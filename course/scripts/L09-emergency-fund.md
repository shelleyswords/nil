# L09 — Your emergency fund

**Lesson ID:** L09
**Module:** 3 — The Simple Foundation

**Learning objective:** Understand what an emergency fund is, why it comes before investing, and the $1,000 starter → 3–6 months target.

**Action step(s):** Set a goal to build a $1,000 starter emergency fund (separate from your tax money), then grow it toward 3–6 months of basic expenses.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

Investing is exciting. I get it — you want to get to the part where your money grows. [pause] We're almost there, I promise. But if you skip the foundation, one bad week can wipe out everything you built. So let's lay the foundation first. It's just a few steps, and step one is your **emergency fund.**

What is it? [pause] An emergency fund is simply a stash of cash set aside for life's surprises. Not for a vacation. Not for a sale. For the *uh-oh* moments. [pause] A flight home for a family thing. A cracked phone. A car repair. A surprise medical bill. The stuff that *always* eventually happens — you just never know when.

Here's why it has to come *before* investing. [pause] If you've got no cushion and an emergency hits, you've only got two bad options. Option one: go into debt to cover it — hello, credit card at twenty percent interest. Option two: yank money out of your investments at the worst possible time, maybe selling at a loss. [pause] The emergency fund is what saves you from *both.* It's the buffer between a small problem and a financial disaster.

So how much? Two targets — a starter and a real one. [pause]

The starter: **one thousand dollars.** That's the first goal, and for a lot of life's surprises, a grand handles it. Get that thousand parked and you've already built a real safety net most people your age don't have.

The bigger target, over time: **three to six months of your basic expenses.** [pause] Meaning — add up what it actually costs you to live for a month. Rent, food, phone, gas, the essentials. Multiply by three to six. *That's* your full emergency fund goal. If you spend, say, fifteen hundred a month to live, you're building toward somewhere between forty-five hundred and nine thousand. You don't get there overnight — you build toward it. But a fully-funded emergency fund means you could lose every deal tomorrow and still be totally fine for months. That's real peace of mind.

Two quick rules so you do this right. [pause] **One: keep it separate from your tax money.** That tax account from Module 2 is *spoken for* — it's the government's. Your emergency fund is a *different* pot. Don't blur them. **Two: keep it boring and reachable.** This money isn't supposed to grow or impress anyone. It's supposed to sit there, safe, ready the instant you need it. We do *not* invest the emergency fund.

Where do you actually keep it so it's safe but still earns a little something? [pause] Great question — that's literally the next lesson, the high-yield savings account. For now, your move is just the goal: start with one thousand dollars, separate from taxes, then build toward three-to-six months. Let's go open the right account for it.
