# L18 — Friends and family who want your money

**Lesson ID:** L18
**Module:** 5 — Protect Yourself

**Learning objective:** Navigate the hardest money situations — loved ones asking for loans or investments — without risking your future or your relationships.

**Action step(s):** Decide your personal "money I'd be fine never seeing again" limit, and prepare a graceful way to say no.

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

The scams from last lesson? Those are the *easy* ones. [pause] A stranger in your DMs — delete, done, no guilt. This lesson is about the *hard* one. The money situation that actually keeps people up at night: when the person asking for your money is someone you *love.*

It happens to basically everyone who comes into money. [pause] A cousin with a business that "just needs a little investment." A best friend with a startup that "literally cannot miss." A family member who needs a loan to get through a rough patch. [pause] And here's what makes it so hard — these aren't scammers. They're people you care about, people who maybe helped *you* get here. So saying no feels like betrayal. That's exactly why this one's dangerous.

Let me give you a framework that protects both your money *and* the relationship. [pause]

First, the mindset: **it is okay to help people. It is NOT okay to risk your future on it.** [pause] Those two things can both be true. You can be generous *and* protect yourself. Generosity does not mean handing over money you need for your taxes, your emergency fund, your retirement. Helping someone shouldn't mean *un-helping* future you.

So here's the simple rule that makes it manageable: **only ever give or "invest" money you'd be completely fine never seeing again.** [pause] Read that carefully. Not money you *hope* to get back. Not money you're "lending." Money you could wave goodbye to forever and genuinely be okay. Because here's the hard reality — a lot of money that goes to a friend's business or a family loan... does not come back. So if you decide up front that you're fine never seeing it, then whatever happens, your finances *and* the friendship survive. [pause] And — this is non-negotiable — **never, ever touch your foundation** for this. Not the tax money, not the emergency fund, not the retirement account. Those are off-limits, no exceptions, no matter who's asking.

Now — the part people struggle with most: *actually saying no.* [pause] So let me give you permission and a script. First, permission: **"No" is a complete sentence.** You do not owe anyone a five-paragraph justification for protecting your own future. [pause] But if a flat no feels too hard, here's a graceful move — **blame the plan, not the person.** Something like: *"Man, I'd love to, but my money's all locked into a long-term setup right now — I literally can't touch it."* [pause] And the beautiful thing is, if you've followed this course... that's *true.* Your money really *is* in your tax account, your emergency fund, your Roth. It really *is* locked into a plan. You're not making an excuse — you're just telling them how you operate. That takes the sting out of it for everybody.

So your move, two parts. [pause] One: decide *your* number — the "money I'd be totally fine never seeing again" limit. Could be a hundred bucks, could be more, but decide it *before* anyone asks, when your head is clear. Two: never cross it, and *never* dip into your foundation. [pause]

That keeps you generous *and* protected — and keeps the people you love from accidentally costing you your future. Next, let's make sure the *deals* you sign are protecting you too. Time to read a contract.
