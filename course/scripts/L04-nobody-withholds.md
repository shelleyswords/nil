# L04 — Why nobody withholds your taxes

**Lesson ID:** L04
**Module:** 2 — Taxes First

**Learning objective:** Understand why NIL income arrives whole (untaxed) and that you are responsible for the taxes yourself — the core surprise of self-employment income.

**Action step(s):** Accept the mental model: "The full amount that landed is NOT all mine." Get ready to set money aside (next lesson).

**Runtime estimate:** ~4 min

---

## Voiceover script

This is the module that saves you from a genuine nightmare. So even if taxes sound boring — stay with me. This is the one that can actually *hurt* you if you skip it. [pause]

Let's start with the thing nobody warns you about: **NIL income is taxable, and nobody withholds the taxes for you.**

Here's why that's such a trap. Think about a normal job — a summer job, a part-time gig. [pause] You make, say, a thousand dollars. But your paycheck isn't a thousand. It's like seven-fifty. Where'd the rest go? Your employer quietly took out taxes *before* the money ever reached you and sent it to the government on your behalf. You never saw it, so you never missed it. By April, your taxes were basically already paid.

NIL money does *not* work like that. [pause]

When a brand pays you, they usually send the **whole amount.** Picture a two-thousand-dollar deal — two thousand dollars lands in your account. All of it. Nothing taken out. And it *feels* like all two thousand is yours. [pause] It is not.

Because you're not an employee in this situation — you're basically **self-employed.** The IRS treats you like a one-person business. And a business is responsible for setting aside and paying *its own* taxes. There's no employer doing it for you anymore. That job is now... yours.

So the money arrives whole, you spend it like it's all yours, and then — months later — tax time shows up and the government wants its share of money you've already spent. [pause] *That's* the nightmare. People owing thousands they no longer have. It happens constantly, and it's one hundred percent avoidable.

Let me make it concrete. [pause] That two-thousand-dollar deal? Realistically, somewhere around five or six hundred of it isn't yours — it belongs to taxes. We'll nail down the exact number next lesson. The point right now is just the mindset shift: **the full amount that hit your account is not the amount you get to keep.**

If you only take one thing from this lesson, take that. The money arrives whole, but it's not *whole yours.* Once you really get that — deep in your bones — the rest of this module is easy. You just set the right amount aside the day it lands, and tax season becomes a total non-event.

Next up: exactly *how much* to set aside, and the dead-simple system to do it automatically. Let's go.
