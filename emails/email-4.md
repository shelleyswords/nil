**BODY**

Foundation's set. Now let's make your money grow.

Here's the secret nobody selling you something wants you to hear: **good investing is supposed to be boring.** Boring wins. "Exciting" is how people lose money.

You don't need to pick winning stocks. You don't need to watch the market. You need exactly two things.

**1. An index fund (the *what* you buy)**

Betting on one company is risky — it can crash and take your money with it. An **index fund** does the opposite: it buys a tiny slice of *all* the big companies at once.

A fund tracking the "total stock market" or the "S&P 500" (the 500 biggest U.S. companies) is the classic core. When the overall economy grows over time, so does your money. It's automatically spread out, and it's nearly free to own.

The wild part? This quietly **beats most expensive professional stock-pickers** over time. You win by being cheap and patient.

Quick rule: before buying any fund, check its **expense ratio** (the fund's yearly fee). A great index fund charges around **0.03%–0.10%**. Anything over 0.20%, keep looking.

**2. A Roth IRA (the *where* you hold it)**

*Where* you keep your investments changes how much tax you pay on the growth. For most young athletes, the best starter account is a **Roth IRA**:

- You put in money you've *already* paid tax on, and then **all the growth comes out tax-free, forever.**
- Because you're young, decades of tax-free compounding is worth an enormous amount.

One thing people mix up: a Roth IRA isn't an investment itself. It's a *bucket* you open at a brokerage — then you buy the index fund *inside* it.

So the whole playbook is:
1. Open a Roth IRA at a big, low-cost brokerage ($0 to open).
2. Buy a broad, low-cost index fund inside it.
3. Add money automatically every month — no matter what the market's doing.
4. Don't touch it. Dips are normal. Time *in* the market beats timing the market.

That's it. That simple system, run for years, builds real wealth.
