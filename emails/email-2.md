**BODY**

Yesterday: taxes. Today: the thing that quietly costs athletes even more.

It's fees. And the scary part is you never see them happen.

A financial advisor who charges **1% a year** sounds like nothing, right? One percent. Who cares.

Here's why you should care.

That 1% comes off your **entire balance, every single year, forever** — whether they make you money or not. Worse, it's 1% of money that *would have grown*. Every dollar skimmed in fees is a dollar that never compounds into more.

Let's put real numbers on it.

Invest steadily starting in your early 20s, and that "tiny" 1% annual fee can cost you **$300,000 to $500,000+** by the time you retire — compared to a near-free index fund holding the exact same investments.

Read that again. Same investments. Same market. The *only* difference is the fee.

That's a house. Gone. Skimmed away 1% at a time, so slowly you'd never notice.

This is why advisors love charging a percentage of your money. It's invisible. They don't mail you a bill — they just quietly take it out of your account while you're not looking.

Here's how you protect yourself. Before you let *anyone* manage a dollar of your money, ask them one question:

**"Are you a fee-only fiduciary, and how exactly do you get paid?"**

- *Fiduciary* = legally required to act in your best interest.
- *Fee-only* = they get paid by you directly (a flat fee or hourly), not by selling you stuff.

If their answer is fuzzy, vague, or they dodge the dollar amount — walk away. That fuzziness is the whole game.
