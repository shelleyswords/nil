**PREVIEW TEXT:** The red flags, the "friends with a deal" trap, and one line that gets you out of anything.

---

**BODY**

Money attracts people. The more visible you get, the more they show up. Today is about keeping your money — and your name — safe.

**Spot the scams.** These red flags should make you run, every time:

- **"Guaranteed" returns.** No legitimate investment guarantees high returns. None. Ever.
- **Pressure to act *right now*.** Urgency is a manipulation tactic. A real opportunity can wait a day.
- **You don't understand it.** Confusion is a sales tool. If they can't explain it in plain English, that's on purpose.
- **They slid into your DMs.** Unsolicited "investment opportunities," crypto coaches, and "I help athletes" cold messages? Delete. Assume every one is a scam until proven otherwise.

**The "friends and family with a deal" trap.** This is the hard one, because it's not strangers — it's people you love. A cousin's business that needs investment. A buddy's can't-miss startup. A family member who needs a loan.

Helping people is fine. Risking your future on it is not. So adopt one simple rule:

**Only "invest" money you'd be totally okay never seeing again — and never your foundation** (tax money, emergency fund, retirement).

And remember: "No" is a complete sentence. If you need an easy out, you can even blame your plan: *"My money's all locked into a long-term setup, I can't touch it."*

**Read before you sign.** NIL deals are contracts. Watch for exclusivity (does it block you from other brands?), length (how long are you locked in?), and what you actually have to deliver. If a deal is more than pocket change, pay someone who knows contracts to look at it first. A short paid review beats a bad multi-year commitment.

Save this question in your notes — it's the one that protects you from almost everyone:

*"Are you a fee-only fiduciary, and how exactly do you get paid?"*
